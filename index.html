The specialism

Camouflage is colour science

A scar is not simply white. It is a different colour to the skin beside it, in a different undertone, on tissue that holds pigment differently. Matching it takes a mix, a test on your skin, and patience across sessions — which is why most artists do not offer it.

  • Stretch marks
  • C-section and surgical scars
  • Vitiligo and lost pigment
  • Scalp camouflage
  • Skin grafts and burn scars
  • Tummy tuck and liposuction
  • Old marks from injury
  • Areas after mastectomy

What to expect

The first month, day by day

Colour looks strongest on day one and settles over four weeks. Knowing the sequence is what stops people panicking on day three.

Day 0

Freshly done

Colour sits 30–40% darker than the healed result. Slight swelling on lips is normal.

Day 2–3

Darkest point

The pigment oxidises and looks bold. This is the stage clients worry about. It passes.

Day 4–7

Flaking

A fine layer lifts away on its own. Do not pick it — it takes pigment with it.

Day 8–14

The grey phase

New skin covers the pigment and the colour looks patchy and faded. It comes back.

Day 28

Settled

True colour is visible. This is when we book the touch-up and correct anything uneven.

The touch-up sits four to eight weeks after the first session. It is part of the treatment, not an upsell: the first pass builds the base, the second sets the final colour.

Before you book

Common questions

Does it hurt?

Topical anaesthetic goes on before and during the session. Most clients describe brows as scratching and lips as a warm buzzing. Camouflage on the body is usually the easiest of the three.

Skin is more sensitive in the week before a period — worth planning around.

How long does it last?

Powder brows one to two years, lip blush one to three, depending on skin type, sun exposure and skincare. Camouflage fades slowly and is topped up when needed.

Acids, retinol and strong sun shorten all of it.

How soon after surgery can a scar be camouflaged?

Usually at least twelve months, and only once your surgeon or doctor confirms the scar is fully mature, closed and no longer changing. Working too early can affect how it settles.
