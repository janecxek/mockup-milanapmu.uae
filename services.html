Services

Brows, lips, lashline, camouflage

Four treatments. Each one starts with your undertone and ends with a touch-up that sets the final colour.

Brows

Powder brows

A soft gradient that reads as light powder, not as a drawn line.

The shape is mapped on your face first, measured against your bone structure and your natural brow, then adjusted until you are happy with the outline drawn on. Nothing is pigmented until you have seen it in a mirror and said yes.

Colour is mixed to sit just under your hair at the front and slightly deeper at the tail, so the brow has direction instead of reading as a block. It looks strongest for the first three days, then settles into the tone you approved.

Good for

  • Sparse or over-plucked brows
  • Brows that disappear without makeup
  • Asymmetry you correct by hand every morning
  • Oily skin, where microblading strokes blur

Lips

Lip blush

Colour worked into the lip itself — definition without a lipstick edge.

Lip blush corrects three things at once: an uneven natural tone, a border that has softened over the years, and a shape that is fuller on one side than the other. The pigment is translucent, so the result moves with your lips rather than sitting on top of them.

Lips swell for about a day and go through a dry, flaky stage in the first week. The procedure can trigger cold sores — if you get them, arrange antiviral cover before the session and tell me at the consultation.

Good for

  • Pale or uneven lip colour
  • A border that has lost its definition
  • Slight asymmetry between the two sides
  • Wanting colour without daily makeup

Eyes

Lashline

Pigment set between the lashes — the eye looks more open, with nothing drawn on the lid.

The pigment goes into the gaps between your own lashes, so the line of growth reads denser and darker than it is. It stops where the lashes stop: there is no flick, no wing and no line sitting on the skin above them.

It is the quietest of the four treatments and the one people notice without being able to say why. Because the area is small and the skin there is thin, the session is shorter than brows or lips, and the pigment settles within the first week.

Good for

  • Sparse or pale lashes that disappear without mascara
  • Wanting definition that survives the gym and the pool
  • Eyes that read tired in photographs
  • Anyone who wants less than a full eyeliner

Camouflage

Scar & stretch mark camouflage

Making a mark stop catching the eye — by matching it, not covering it.

Camouflage tattooing places skin-toned pigment into a mature scar, stretch mark or area of lost pigment so it blends with the skin around it. It does not remove the scar and it does not change its texture. What it removes is the colour contrast that makes your eye land there.

The work runs across sessions, usually two to four, eight to twelve weeks apart. Scar tissue holds pigment unpredictably, so each session is planned from how the last one healed rather than from a fixed plan.

Not every scar is suitable. Raised, keloid or still-red scars are not treated, and a patch test on a small area comes before any large one is started.

Good for

  • White, mature stretch marks on hips, thighs or chest
  • C-section, tummy tuck and mastectomy scars
  • Vitiligo and other areas of lost pigment
  • Skin grafts and healed burn scars
  • Scalp camouflage over scars in the hairline
  • Old marks left by injury or surgery
